Brian

Privacy policy

Last updated 6 November 2026

Brian is a notes and study app made by Celix ("we", "us"). This page explains what Brian collects, why, who it's shared with, and how to get it deleted. We are the data controller for the personal data described here.

The short version: much of Brian works on your phone without an account. If you sign in, your notes sync through our cloud database. When you ask Brian a question in the cloud, the question and the notes it needs go to an AI model to write the answer. Ads only play when you choose to watch one. We don't sell your data, and we don't use your notes to pick ads.

Using Brian without an account

Without signing in, what you write, capture and import stays on your phone. On iPhones that support Apple Intelligence, Ask can run entirely on the device, and those questions never leave it.

What we collect when you sign in

  • Account details. You sign in with Apple. Apple gives us an account identifier and an email address, which can be Apple's private relay address if you choose to hide yours.
  • What you keep in Brian. Projects, pages, tasks, captures, milestones and trackers, and the changes you make to them. These are stored in our database so they sync between your devices and are backed up.
  • Devices. A record of each device signed in to your account, so changes sync to the right places.
  • Usage counts. How many cloud questions you ask each day or week, so fair-use limits work. We count questions; we don't keep the questions themselves in this count.
  • Plan details. Which plan you're on and, for Brian Plus, a record of the ads you finished. These are used to unlock questions and to stop abuse.

Your Ask conversations are kept on your phone and are not synced to our database.

Asking Brian questions in the cloud

When you ask a question using the cloud rather than on-device Ask, we send your question to an AI model. With it go the parts of your notes, tasks, deadlines and lecture files Brian needs to answer, and any photo or file you attach. The request passes through our server to OpenRouter, which routes it to an AI provider (currently DeepSeek's models, served by providers OpenRouter selects). They process it to write the answer. We don't use your questions to train AI models, and we don't sell them.

Your university's rules on AI

Some universities don't allow course materials, such as lecture slides, recordings or internal documents, to be put into AI tools. Brian only sends what you choose to ask about, so following your university's rules on this is your responsibility. If your university doesn't allow it, or you'd rather nothing left your phone, use on-device Ask on an iPhone that supports Apple Intelligence. Everything then stays on the device. You can also simply not ask cloud questions about those files.

Your university

If you connect your university's learning platform (such as Brightspace or Moodle), you sign in on your university's own page inside Brian. Your password goes to your university, not to us. Brian keeps the university's sign-in cookies on your phone so you stay signed in. It then downloads your modules, deadlines, announcements and lecture files to your phone. Deadlines and announcements become items in Brian, which sync like everything else if you're signed in. Lecture files stay on your phone, except for the passages sent to the AI when you ask about them.

Google, GitHub and other connections

If you connect Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Contacts, Google Drive or GitHub, the sign-in tokens are kept in your phone's secure storage, and your phone fetches the data directly from those services. Brian uses that data only to show it to you and to answer questions you ask about it; then it's sent to the AI as described above. It is never used for advertising and never sold. You can disconnect any service in Brian, and revoke access in your Google or GitHub account settings.

Camera, photos and notifications

The camera is used to scan the sign-in code for Brian on the web, and to take photos you attach. Brian only reads the photos you pick. Reminders are scheduled on your phone; we don't send push notifications through a server.

Ads (Brian Plus)

Brian Plus is free and kept by watching a short ad break once a week; extra ads add questions. Ads only play when you tap to watch one, and are provided by Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects information from your device. That includes device and app information, your IP address, approximate location, and how you interact with the ad. Google may combine this with other information it holds. When you finish an ad, Google tells our server, using your Brian account identifier, so we can credit the reward.

If you're in the UK, the EEA or Switzerland, Brian asks for your consent with Google's consent form before the first ad. You can change your choice any time in Brian under Account → Plan → Ad privacy choices. Brian doesn't ask iOS for permission to track you across other companies' apps, so ads on iPhone aren't based on that tracking.

Read how Google uses information from apps that use its services. Brian Pro has no ads.

Payments (Brian Pro)

If you buy Brian Pro, payment is handled by Stripe. We receive whether your subscription is active, never your card number.

Who we share data with

We share personal data only with the services that run Brian, and only for that purpose:

  • Supabase: our database, sign-in and server functions, hosted in Frankfurt, Germany.
  • OpenRouter and the AI providers it routes to: cloud answers.
  • Google AdMob: ads in Brian Plus.
  • Apple: Sign in with Apple, and on-device Apple Intelligence.
  • Stripe: Brian Pro payments.

Some of these process data outside the UK, including in the United States. Where they do, transfers rely on the UK's adequacy regulations or standard contractual clauses. We also disclose data if the law requires it. We don't sell personal data.

Why we use it (legal bases)

  • To provide Brian: your account, sync, Ask and connections (performance of our contract with you).
  • Personalised ads in the UK and EEA: your consent, which you can withdraw at any time.
  • Security, fair-use limits and preventing ad fraud: our legitimate interest in keeping Brian running and affordable.

How long we keep it

We keep your account data while your account exists. Usage counts and ad records are kept for as long as they're needed for limits and fraud checks, and are deleted with your account. Data on your phone stays until you delete it. In Brian, Account → Erase all content and settings removes everything Brian keeps on the phone.

Deleting your account

Erasing in the app clears your phone but keeps the cloud copy. To delete your account and everything we store for it, contact us using the details below, from or mentioning the account's email address. We'll delete it within 30 days.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of your data, and have it corrected or deleted. You can also object to or restrict how we use it, and take it to another service. You can export your data yourself in Brian under Account → Export a backup. To use any of these rights, contact us. If you're unhappy with how we handle your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

Children

Brian is made for students at university and college, and isn't directed at children under 13.

Changes

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top. If a change matters, we'll also say so in the app.
